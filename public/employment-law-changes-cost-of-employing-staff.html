Employing Someone? The Law Just Changed Your Costs

By John James · Founder of ProfitQuote · 5 min read · Last updated: 25 November 2026

Every quote you send assumes your costs stay put. The Employment Rights Act 2025 has just moved some of them. If you employ a labourer, an apprentice or an office person, sick pay now starts on day one, new harassment duties have started, and from 1 January 2027 the rules on dismissal change. If your prices do not allow for that, you are paying for it out of your profit.

The short version

Sick pay from day one (since 6 April 2026) → harassment duties (since 30 October 2026) → easier unfair dismissal claims (from 1 January 2027).

What has changed

These changes come from the Employment Rights Act 2025, which is being phased in across 2026 and 2027.

ChangeStartsWhat it means
Statutory Sick Pay6 April 2026No waiting days and no lower earnings limit. Paid from the first day of sickness absence.
Tribunal time limit1 October 2026Most employment tribunal claims: 6 months instead of 3.
Harassment duties30 October 2026“All reasonable steps” to prevent sexual harassment, and no permitting harassment of staff by customers or clients.
Unfair dismissal1 January 2027Shorter qualifying period (six months, instead of two years) and no cap on the compensatory award.
Sources: GOV.UK, Employment Rights changes (business.gov.uk); Acas, Statutory sick pay and Employment Rights Act 2025 pages.

Who this applies to

If you work completely alone, most of this does not apply to you directly. It applies when you employ people. Statutory Sick Pay is for people who are classed as employed for tax purposes. Acas says workers who pay their own tax through self assessment are not eligible, so a self-employed subcontractor is not entitled to it from you.

The government is also consulting on employment status reform and protections for the self-employed, with views due by 8 January 2027, so it is worth keeping an eye on.

What it does to your costs

Eligible workers get whichever is lower: £123.25 a week, or 80% of their average weekly earnings over the 8 weeks before the absence. The sick pay itself is only part of the cost. The bigger bill is usually the job that runs late, or the cover you pay for while someone is off.

None of that appears on a materials list, which is exactly how it goes missing from quotes. It is an overhead, and it needs to be recovered like one. If you have never worked out what your overheads add up to, start with our guide to calculating construction overheads.

How to build it into your day rate

Absence allowance per working day

(sick days you expect × cost of each sick day) ÷ working days in the year

Illustration only, with made-up numbers. Say you allow for 5 sick days per employee each year, and each one costs you £150 in sick pay, cover and lost output. That is £750 a year. Divided by 225 working days, it comes to about £3.33 per working day. That is the amount to build into the day rate you charge for that person.

Use your own figures, not these. The point is that a small, steady allowance in every quote is far cheaper than finding £750 in a bad month. For the full method, see our guide to calculating your profit margin and 25 costs tradespeople forget to include in their quotes.

Coming on 1 January 2027

GOV.UK says unfair dismissal protections change from 1 January 2027. The qualifying period for an ordinary unfair dismissal claim falls from two years to six months, and the cap on the compensatory award is removed. In practice, anyone who has already worked for you for six months will be covered from that date.

That is not a cost you can put in a quote. It does raise the price of getting people management wrong, so it is worth having written contracts, dated notes of performance and conduct, and a call to Acas before you dismiss anyone.

Source: GOV.UK, Employment Rights changes (business.gov.uk).

Harassment duties since 30 October 2026

GOV.UK says employers must now take “all reasonable steps” to prevent sexual harassment of their employees, and must not permit harassment of their employees by third parties such as customers and clients. These measures apply in England, Wales and Scotland. If your people work in other people's homes, write down how someone reports a problem and what you will do about it.

Source: GOV.UK, Workplace sexual harassment and third-party harassment (business.gov.uk).

This guide is general information, not legal advice. Dates and rules can change, so check the official GOV.UK guidance or call Acas before you act.
